# 1. Purpose

This document reconciles the approved logical data model established by P2-02.2 into explicit physical-design requirements.

It is an intermediate control artifact between:

    Approved Logical Model
            ↓
    Physical Requirements
            ↓
    Physical Design Decisions
            ↓
    Physical Data Design

Its purpose is to ensure that physical design decisions are derived from approved logical requirements rather than from database technology preferences.

This document does not select a database technology.

It does not define the final physical schema.

It establishes the requirements that the physical design must satisfy.

# 3. Reconciliation Principle

The physical design must satisfy the approved logical model.

The direction is:

    Governance Standards
            ↓
    Platform Architecture
            ↓
    Data-Layer Architecture
            ↓
    Approved Logical Model
            ↓
    Physical Requirements
            ↓
    Physical Design

Physical requirements must not redefine logical semantics.

Where a physical requirement cannot be satisfied without changing the logical model, the issue must be escalated rather than silently resolved through physical implementation.

# 6. Requirement Group A — Knowledge Asset Identity

## A-01 Stable Identity

The physical representation must provide stable identity for each Knowledge Asset.

The identifier must remain stable through:

- content revision;
- version creation;
- review;
- approval;
- publication;
- downstream representation.

## A-02 Identity Independence

The physical identifier must not depend solely on:

- title;
- URL;
- language;
- publication format;
- page location;
- mutable content.

## A-03 Physical Identifier Mechanism

The mechanism remains open.

Candidate mechanisms may include:

- UUID;
- ULID;
- database-generated integer;
- another justified stable identifier.

The physical mechanism must preserve logical identity semantics.

---

# 7. Requirement Group B — Knowledge Asset Version

## B-01 Independent Version Addressability

Each Knowledge Asset Version must be independently addressable.

The physical design must permit retrieval of:

- the parent Knowledge Asset;
- a specific version;
- the version sequence;
- its lifecycle state;
- its governed historical state.

## B-02 Parent Relationship

Every Knowledge Asset Version must maintain an unambiguous relationship to its parent Knowledge Asset.

## B-03 Version Uniqueness

A version must be uniquely distinguishable within its parent Knowledge Asset.

## B-04 Version Ordering

Versions must have an ordering mechanism.

The exact numbering convention remains open.

## B-05 Historical Preservation

Creation of a new version must not overwrite the historical representation of an earlier governed version.

---

# 8. Requirement Group C — Current Version Resolution

## C-01 Current Version

The physical design must provide an efficient method for resolving the current governed version of a Knowledge Asset.

## C-02 Historical Independence

Current-version resolution must not modify historical version identity or content.

## C-03 Single Current Published Version

The baseline logical rule is one current published version per Knowledge Asset.

The physical design must support this rule unless a future approved governance requirement changes it.

---

# 9. Requirement Group D — Version Immutability

## D-01 Approved Version Protection

Once a Knowledge Asset Version reaches an approved governed state, its substantive semantic representation must be protected from destructive modification.

## D-02 New Version for Substantive Change

A substantive knowledge change must be represented through a new Knowledge Asset Version.

## D-03 Physical Enforcement

The physical design must determine which aspects of immutability can be enforced through:

- database constraints;
- permissions;
- transaction rules;
- application-level governance;
- other controlled mechanisms.

The database must not be expected to enforce rules that cannot reasonably be expressed at the database layer.

---

# 10. Requirement Group E — Lifecycle

## E-01 Lifecycle Representation

The physical design must represent the approved P2-02.2 lifecycle:

- Draft
- In Review
- Approved
- Published
- Superseded
- Retired

## E-02 Lifecycle Authority

P2-02.2 is the approved logical baseline.

Earlier conceptual lifecycle descriptions must not silently introduce additional physical lifecycle states.

## E-03 Lifecycle History

Material lifecycle transitions must remain reconstructable.

## E-04 Invalid States

The physical design must reduce the possibility of invalid lifecycle states through appropriate constraints and controlled transaction/application logic.

---

# 11. Requirement Group F — Taxonomy Concept

## F-01 Stable Concept Identity

Each Taxonomy Concept requires stable identity independent of:

- language;
- spelling;
- title;
- URL;
- publication;
- downstream representation.

## F-02 Concept Type

Concept Type is governed and must not be unrestricted arbitrary text where controlled physical representation is appropriate.

## F-03 Concept Status

Concept governance status must be physically representable.

## F-04 Taxonomy Relationships

Parent/child and other taxonomy classification relationships must be explicitly representable.

## F-05 Concept Language

Multiple language representations must be capable of belonging to the same conceptual identity.

## F-06 MTS Boundary

Physical design must not introduce a generic `MedicalConcept` entity merely for implementation convenience.

---

# 12. Requirement Group G — Multilingual Representation

## G-01 Identity Independence

Language must not determine governed identity.

## G-02 Language Representation

Physical design must provide a structured mechanism for language-specific representations where required.

## G-03 Preferred Terms

Preferred terms must remain distinguishable from:

- synonyms;
- alternative terms;
- abbreviations;
- historical terms.

## G-04 Future Retrieval

The physical design should support future multilingual retrieval without requiring redesign of core identity structures.

Search implementation remains outside P2-02.3.

---

# 13. Requirement Group H — Knowledge Representation

## H-01 Structured Knowledge

The physical design must support structured knowledge representation.

## H-02 No Generic Content Collapse

The physical design must not reduce governed knowledge to an uncontrolled generic `content` field.

## H-03 Controlled Flexibility

Flexible structured storage may be considered where justified.

It must not replace explicit structures where governed integrity is required.

## H-04 Schema Evolution

The physical design must identify how structured knowledge representation can evolve safely.

## H-05 Open Logical Scope

P2-02.2 leaves detailed Knowledge Representation Structure as an open decision.

Physical design must not invent a detailed content ontology without the required governing input.

---

# 14. Requirement Group I — Relationships

## I-01 Explicit Relationships

Governed relationships must be physically represented explicitly.

## I-02 Relationship Integrity

Relationships must preserve:

- endpoint identity;
- relationship semantics;
- direction where applicable;
- cardinality where applicable;
- relevant metadata.

## I-03 No Uncontrolled ID Arrays

Governed relationships must not be represented solely as uncontrolled embedded identifier lists where stronger integrity is required.

## I-04 Semantic Relationship Versioning

Relationships that materially constitute or qualify governed knowledge must remain reconstructable with the applicable Knowledge Asset Version.

## I-05 Non-Versioned Relationships

Contextual, navigational, taxonomy-classification, independently governed, or derived relationships must not automatically force Knowledge Asset version creation.

---

# 15. Requirement Group J — Evidence and Provenance

## J-01 Evidence Traceability

The physical design must preserve the ability to determine which evidence supports or contradicts governed knowledge.

## J-02 Source Separation

Evidence and Evidence Source must remain distinguishable.

## J-03 Provenance

Evidence relationships must preserve sufficient provenance to support historical reconstruction.

## J-04 Historical Evidence State

Historical Knowledge Asset Versions must be capable of being reconstructed with the evidence/provenance state applicable to their governed state.

## J-05 Conflicting Evidence

The physical model must allow conflicting, supporting, or partially supporting evidence to coexist where governance requires it.

## J-06 Evidence Model Boundary

Detailed Evidence, Evidence Claim, Evidence Body, Evidence Source, Evidence Strength, and Knowledge Confidence structures remain partially open under P2-02.2.

Physical design must not prematurely invent unsupported detailed evidence structures.

---

# 16. Requirement Group K — Governance

## K-01 Approval

Approval must be represented as an explicit governance event or governed decision.

## K-02 Review

Review information must be separately representable from substantive knowledge content.

## K-03 Governance History

Material governance events must remain reconstructable.

## K-04 Current-State Independence

Historical governance cannot depend solely on mutable current-state fields.

## K-05 Audit Boundary

Audit information must be sufficient to reconstruct material governance history without becoming an uncontrolled duplicate of every database operation.

---

# 17. Requirement Group L — Integrity Constraints

The physical design must identify appropriate enforcement for:

- primary identity;
- foreign-key integrity;
- uniqueness;
- nullability;
- version uniqueness;
- version ordering;
- lifecycle values;
- relationship integrity;
- required governance references.

Each proposed constraint must be classified as:

1. Domain rule
2. Governance rule
3. Data-integrity rule
4. Implementation convenience

Implementation convenience alone is not sufficient justification for an authoritative constraint.

---

# 18. Requirement Group M — Indexing

The physical design must evaluate indexes against actual expected access patterns.

Initial access patterns include:

1. Knowledge Asset lookup by stable identity
2. Knowledge Asset Version lookup
3. Current-version resolution
4. Taxonomy Concept lookup
5. Taxonomy hierarchy traversal
6. Relationship retrieval
7. Lifecycle filtering
8. Governance filtering
9. Evidence retrieval
10. Administrative queries

Each major index must have a documented purpose.

No speculative index should be introduced merely because the database supports it.

---

# 19. Requirement Group N — Transactions

The physical design must identify atomic operations.

At minimum, evaluate:

- version creation;
- version submission for review;
- approval;
- publication state change;
- supersession;
- relationship creation where governed integrity requires atomicity;
- taxonomy updates where multiple structures must remain consistent.

The design should prefer simple transactional boundaries.

---

# 20. Requirement Group O — Concurrency

The physical design must consider:

- simultaneous editing;
- simultaneous review;
- concurrent approval;
- concurrent version creation;
- taxonomy updates;
- governance updates.

It must prevent or appropriately control:

- duplicate versions;
- conflicting current-version resolution;
- stale writes;
- invalid state transitions;
- inconsistent governance state.

The selected mechanism must be proportionate to actual requirements.

---

# 21. Requirement Group P — Migration

The physical design must support controlled schema evolution.

Requirements include:

- versioned migrations;
- reproducible migration ordering;
- environment consistency;
- destructive-change controls;
- rollback or recovery strategy;
- reference-data migration;
- taxonomy migration where required.

Migration tooling remains a physical implementation decision.

---

# 22. Requirement Group Q — Initialization

Initialization must distinguish:

### Q-01 Schema

Physical structures required by the application.

### Q-02 Reference Data

Controlled operational data such as:

- controlled vocabularies;
- lifecycle values;
- taxonomy bootstrap/reference structures;
- system configuration.

### Q-03 Governed Knowledge

Actual Knowledge Assets and Knowledge Asset Versions.

Governed knowledge must not be treated as ordinary database seed data.

---

# 23. Requirement Group R — Operational Requirements

The physical design must consider:

- backup;
- restoration;
- transaction integrity;
- data consistency;
- monitoring;
- retention;
- historical preservation.

Detailed production infrastructure remains outside P2-02.3 where it belongs to later infrastructure/operations work.

---

# 24. Search Boundary

The physical design must support future search integration without implementing search.

It may provide:

- stable identifiers;
- source identifiers;
- metadata;
- version/change information;
- change-detection signals;
- indexing-relevant timestamps.

It must not implement:

- search ranking;
- semantic search;
- search engine infrastructure;
- embeddings;
- retrieval pipelines.

P2-05 remains authoritative for Search & Retrieval.

---

# 25. AI Boundary

The physical data layer must provide authoritative governed data to future AI systems.

It must not:

- introduce model-specific authoritative structures;
- make embeddings authoritative;
- introduce vector storage without a justified requirement;
- allow AI-generated information to bypass governance.

AI remains a consumer/accelerator of governed knowledge.

It does not define the governed knowledge model.

---

# 26. API Boundary

The physical design must support future API access through:

- stable identifiers;
- predictable relationships;
- version addressability;
- lifecycle filtering;
- governance filtering;
- efficient primary lookups.

It must not define API endpoints or API contracts.

P2-03 remains authoritative for Knowledge API design.

---

# 27. Storage Architecture Requirements

The physical design must evaluate whether one primary persistence technology can satisfy the approved requirements.

Default:

> One primary database unless a concrete requirement demonstrates that additional persistence technology is necessary.

Additional storage technology requires:

1. concrete requirement;
2. demonstrated limitation of the primary store;
3. meaningful benefit;
4. understood operational complexity;
5. defined ownership;
6. defined synchronization;
7. understood failure modes.

No vector database, graph database, document database, search engine, or specialized cache should be introduced merely for future possibilities.

# 30. Important Reconciliation Finding — Lifecycle

P2-02.1 contains an earlier conceptual lifecycle description that includes states such as:

- Monitoring
- Update
- Re-review
- Archive

P2-02.2, however, is the approved logical baseline and explicitly establishes:

- Draft
- In Review
- Approved
- Published
- Superseded
- Retired

Therefore, physical design must use the P2-02.2 approved lifecycle semantics.

The earlier P2-02.1 conceptual lifecycle must not be independently implemented as additional database states.

If future governance requires additional lifecycle states, that change must occur through the appropriate governance/logical-model review.

---

# 31. Important Reconciliation Finding — Evidence

P2-02.2 establishes strong evidence/provenance requirements but leaves detailed structures such as:

- Evidence Claim;
- Evidence Body;
- Evidence Source;
- Evidence Strength;
- Knowledge Confidence;

partially open.

Therefore:

**Physical design must preserve the requirements without prematurely inventing a detailed evidence schema that has not yet been formally defined.**

If implementation detail is required before those logical structures are resolved, the issue must be escalated rather than silently finalized.

---

# 32. Important Reconciliation Finding — Knowledge Representation

P2-02.2 requires structured knowledge and explicitly rejects reducing governed knowledge to a generic `content` field.

However, the detailed internal structure of Knowledge Representation remains open.

Therefore, P2-02.3 physical design must determine only the physical capabilities required to support the approved logical boundary.

It must not independently invent the final content ontology.

---

# 33. Architecture Escalation Conditions

A physical-design decision must be escalated when it would:

- change entity boundaries;
- change logical identity;
- change version semantics;
- change lifecycle semantics;
- change approved relationship semantics;
- introduce a new architectural persistence subsystem;
- create a competing authoritative knowledge store;
- redefine evidence governance;
- redefine taxonomy governance.

Such decisions are not ordinary D1 physical implementation decisions.

---

# 34. Physical Design Test

The physical design must eventually satisfy this test:

> Could a competent engineering team implement the Knowledge Data Layer from the approved physical design without making undocumented architectural or logical-model decisions?

If **No**:

Physical design remains incomplete.

If **Yes**:

The design may proceed to formal architecture review.
