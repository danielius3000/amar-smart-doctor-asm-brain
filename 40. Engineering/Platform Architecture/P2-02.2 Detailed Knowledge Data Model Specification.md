## 1. Purpose

This document defines the logical data-model foundation for the ASM Brain Knowledge Data Layer.

It translates the governing knowledge architecture and approved P2-02 design decisions into a structured logical model while deliberately avoiding premature physical implementation decisions.

This document defines:

- logical entities;
- entity boundaries;
- identity semantics;
- versioning semantics;
- lifecycle semantics;
- core logical attributes;
- entity responsibilities;
- logical relationships;
- governance boundaries.

This document does not define:

- database tables;
- SQL schemas;
- ORM models;
- database-specific data types;
- physical indexes;
- database engine selection;
- search-index implementation;
- vector-storage implementation;
- caching architecture;
- API implementation.

---

# 2. Architectural Principles

The Knowledge Data Layer must preserve the core ASM Brain principle that **knowledge is the primary governed asset**.

Pages, articles, videos, social-media outputs, search results, APIs, and other downstream representations are outputs derived from governed knowledge rather than the authoritative knowledge objects themselves.

The logical data model must therefore preserve:

- structured knowledge;
- stable identity;
- version history;
- evidence traceability;
- provenance;
- relationships;
- governance state;
- lifecycle;
- historical reconstruction.

The model must avoid collapsing distinct responsibilities into a single generic content object.

---

# 3. Logical Entity Model

The foundational logical model contains the following entities:

1. Knowledge Asset
2. Knowledge Asset Version
3. Taxonomy Concept

The broader term **Medical Concept** may be used as a domain concept where appropriate, but the governed logical concept entity used by this model is the **Taxonomy Concept** defined by the Medical Taxonomy Standard (MTS).

---

# 4. Entity Boundaries

## 4.1 Knowledge Asset

### Definition

A Knowledge Asset is the governed, reusable unit of structured medical knowledge within ASM Brain.

Its identity represents the knowledge object itself, independent of a particular version, publication format, page, article, video, social-media representation, or other downstream output.

### Responsibilities

A Knowledge Asset is responsible for:

- stable asset identity;
- asset type;
- canonical name/title;
- governed knowledge representation;
- scope;
- structured knowledge components;
- current governance state;
- current version resolution;
- governed metadata;
- logical relationships to other governed objects.

### It does not represent

A Knowledge Asset is not:

- a website page;
- a blog post;
- a video;
- a social-media post;
- a translation by itself;
- a source document;
- an individual evidence claim;
- a publication channel;
- a database record merely because one exists.

### Boundary principle

**Knowledge Asset = governed knowledge.**

It is not equivalent to a presentation of that knowledge.

---

## 4.2 Knowledge Asset Version

### Definition

A Knowledge Asset Version represents a particular governed state of a Knowledge Asset.

A Knowledge Asset retains stable identity across versions.

### Responsibilities

A Knowledge Asset Version is responsible for:

- version identity;
- parent Knowledge Asset identity;
- version sequence;
- version-specific knowledge representation;
- version-specific scope;
- structured knowledge components;
- change context;
- lifecycle state;
- effective/temporal information;
- historical traceability;
- references to governance structures.

### It does not represent

A Knowledge Asset Version is not:

- an independent Knowledge Asset;
- a separate Medical/Taxonomy Concept;
- a publication channel;
- an evidence object;
- a source;
- a database update record.

### Boundary principle

**Knowledge Asset = which governed knowledge object?**

**Knowledge Asset Version = which governed state of that object?**

---

## 4.3 Medical Concept / Taxonomy Concept

### Terminology clarification

"Medical Concept" is used as a broader domain term.

The actual governed logical concept entity used in this model is the **Taxonomy Concept**, as defined by the Medical Taxonomy Standard (MTS).

This model does not introduce a separate generic `MedicalConcept` entity unless a future governing requirement explicitly requires one.

### Definition

A Taxonomy Concept represents a stable conceptual subject within the governed medical taxonomy.

Examples may include concepts such as:

- conditions;
- symptoms/signs;
- findings;
- anatomical structures;
- procedures/interventions;
- investigations/diagnostic tests;
- medications/therapeutic agents;
- risk factors;
- prevention/health concepts;
- specialties;
- other controlled medical concepts.

### Responsibilities

A Taxonomy Concept is responsible for:

- stable concept identity;
- concept type;
- controlled terminology;
- preferred term;
- definition/description where applicable;
- synonyms and alternative terms;
- multilingual representation;
- concept status;
- taxonomy governance context.

### It does not represent

A Taxonomy Concept is not:

- a Knowledge Asset;
- a Knowledge Asset Version;
- evidence;
- a source;
- a medical claim;
- a webpage;
- SEO content;
- website navigation;
- a publication;
- an audit record.

### Boundary principle

**Taxonomy Concept = the governed medical subject/concept.**

**Knowledge Asset = governed knowledge concerning that subject.**

# 5. Identity Model

## 5.1 Identity Principle

Identity answers:

> What object is this?

Version answers:

> Which governed state of that object is this?

Identity must therefore remain separate from:

- content;
- version;
- publication;
- language representation;
- URL;
- downstream representation.

---

## 5.2 Knowledge Asset Identity

A Knowledge Asset has a stable logical identifier.

The identifier remains stable through:

- content revisions;
- version changes;
- review cycles;
- approval changes;
- publication changes;
- downstream representations.

A substantive revision does not create a new Knowledge Asset identity.

It creates a new version.

---

## 5.3 Knowledge Asset Version Identity

Each version must be independently identifiable.

The logical identity of a version must include its relationship to the parent Knowledge Asset and distinguish the specific governed state.

The combination of:

**Knowledge Asset Identity + Version Identity**

must uniquely identify a governed version.

The exact physical identifier mechanism remains deliberately open.

---

## 5.4 Medical / Taxonomy Concept Identity

A Taxonomy Concept has stable conceptual identity independent of:

- language;
- spelling;
- title;
- webpage URL;
- content format;
- publication channel;
- SEO representation.

Changing a representation does not automatically create a new concept.

---

## 5.5 Taxonomy Identity

A taxonomy concept must have stable identity within its governed taxonomy.

The following remain open pending detailed MTS analysis:

- global versus taxonomy-scoped identifier;
- taxonomy-version mechanics;
- whether taxonomy nodes require separate identity from concepts;
- detailed concept supersession rules.

---

# 6. Identity Rules

### I-01 — Stable identity

A governed entity's logical identity remains stable throughout its lifecycle unless the entity is determined to have been incorrectly identified.

### I-02 — Identity is not content

Changing the content of a Knowledge Asset does not create a new Knowledge Asset identity.

It creates a new version where the change is substantive.

### I-03 — Identity is not publication

Publishing a Knowledge Asset through a website, API, document, video, or other channel does not create a new Knowledge Asset.

### I-04 — Identity is not language

A translated representation does not automatically create a new Knowledge Asset or Taxonomy Concept merely because its language differs.

### I-05 — Version uniqueness

A Knowledge Asset Version must be uniquely identifiable within its parent Knowledge Asset.

### I-06 — Historical addressability

The system must be able to identify and retrieve a specific historical version.

### I-07 — Governed cross-object identity

Logical references between governed objects must use governed logical identities rather than unstable storage identifiers.

### I-08 — No premature physical ID decision

P2-02.2 defines logical identity semantics.

It does not yet prescribe whether implementation will use UUID, ULID, integer sequences, or another physical identifier mechanism.

---

# 7. Versioning & Lifecycle Semantics

## 7.1 Versioning Principle

A Knowledge Asset has one stable identity and may have multiple governed versions.

Conceptually:

    Knowledge Asset
        |
        +-- Version 1
        +-- Version 2
        +-- Version 3
        +-- Version 4

---

## 7.2 Substantive Change Creates a New Version

A change that materially changes the governed knowledge represented by an asset creates a new version.

Examples include:

- changing a medical statement;
- adding or removing substantive knowledge;
- changing an evidence-supported recommendation;
- materially changing scope;
- changing content in a way that affects interpretation.

---

## 7.3 Operational Changes

Not every technical or operational change creates a knowledge version.

Examples that do not automatically create a new knowledge version include:

- internal processing metadata;
- system timestamps;
- search-index updates;
- cache updates;
- access logs;
- unrelated audit events;
- technical implementation changes.

A semantic-neutral typographical correction remains a governance-dependent case and is not yet rigidly defined.

---

# 8. Version Immutability

Once a version becomes an approved governed state, its substantive knowledge representation must be treated as immutable.

A later substantive change creates a new version.

Conceptually:

    Version 1 — Approved
            |
            +-- remains historically preserved
            |
            +-- substantive revision
                      |
                      v
                 Version 2 — Draft

An approved version must not silently become another governed state through destructive editing.

---

# 9. Lifecycle and Version Are Distinct

Lifecycle state and version identity are related but distinct.

### Knowledge Asset

Represents the overall governed object.

### Knowledge Asset Version

Represents a particular governed state.

### Lifecycle

Represents the governance state of that version or asset.

A Knowledge Asset may therefore have:

    Knowledge Asset
    Status: Active

    +-- Version 1 — Superseded
    +-- Version 2 — Published
    +-- Version 3 — Draft

The lifecycle history is not collapsed into the Knowledge Asset's identity.

---

# 10. Version Lifecycle

The logical version lifecycle is:

    Draft
      |
      v
    In Review
      |
      v
    Approved
      |
      v
    Published
      |
      v
    Superseded

An exceptional retirement path may exist:

    Governed Version
          |
          v
       Retired

### Draft

Version is being created or revised.

### In Review

Version has entered formal review.

### Approved

Version has satisfied the required approval criteria.

### Published

Version has been released to one or more downstream consumers.

### Superseded

A later version has replaced this version as the current governed version.

### Retired

The version or asset is intentionally withdrawn according to governance rules.

Retirement does not imply deletion of historical records.

---

# 11. Current Version

"Current Version" is treated as a state or relationship resolved from the parent Knowledge Asset.

It is not a permanent intrinsic property of a historical Version.

Conceptually:

    Knowledge Asset
          |
          +-- current governed version --> Version 4

When Version 5 becomes current:

    Knowledge Asset
          |
          +-- current governed version --> Version 5

Version 4 remains historically addressable and is not rewritten.

---

# 12. Version Numbering

The logical model requires versions to be:

- uniquely distinguishable;
- ordered;
- traceable to their parent Knowledge Asset.

The exact numbering scheme remains open.

P2-02.2 does not yet mandate semantic versioning, numeric versioning, or another physical numbering convention.

---

# 13. Multiple Published Versions

Default rule:

> One current published version per Knowledge Asset.

Future scenarios such as:

- jurisdiction-specific versions;
- language-specific publication;
- regulatory variants;
- controlled audience variants;

must not complicate the base model prematurely.

Any such future requirement should be introduced through an explicitly defined governance dimension.

---

# 14. Knowledge Asset Attributes

## 14.1 Identity Attributes

| Attribute | Required | Nature | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge Asset ID | Yes | Immutable | Stable identity of the asset |
| Asset Type | Yes | Governed | Identifies the kind of Knowledge Asset |
| Canonical Name / Title | Yes | Mutable | Human-readable identity/label |

The Knowledge Asset ID is the authoritative identity.

The title is not the identity.

---

## 14.2 Core Knowledge Definition

The Knowledge Asset must contain the governed knowledge representation.

The logical model therefore recognizes:

- Knowledge Representation;
- Scope;
- Structured Knowledge Components.

The exact internal structure of these components remains subject to further detailed content-model analysis.

The model deliberately avoids reducing governed knowledge to a generic `content` field.

---

## 14.3 Governance State

The Knowledge Asset may expose its current governed state through:

- current version resolution;
- lifecycle state;
- review state;
- approval state;
- authority/governance state.

Detailed lifecycle events, review records, approval records, and audit records remain separate logical structures.

---

## 14.4 Metadata

Governed metadata belongs to the Knowledge Asset model where required by KAS and related standards.

Metadata may include:

- canonical title/name;
- asset type;
- ownership/governance information;
- creation information;
- modification information;
- language-related metadata where applicable;
- governance information;
- publication restrictions where applicable.

Technical implementation metadata such as database identifiers, search-index identifiers, vector IDs, cache keys, and similar implementation artifacts do not belong to the authoritative logical Knowledge Asset model.

---

## 14.5 Relationships

Knowledge Asset relationships remain separate from intrinsic attributes.

Potential logical relationships include:

- Medical/Taxonomy Concepts;
- Taxonomy classification;
- Evidence;
- Sources through evidence;
- other Knowledge Assets;
- Knowledge Relationships;
- Dependencies;
- Provenance;
- Review structures;
- Approval structures.

These must not be collapsed into authoritative embedded ID lists inside the Knowledge Asset.

---

# 15. Knowledge Asset Version Attributes

## 15.1 Identity Attributes

| Attribute | Required | Nature | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge Asset Version Identity | Yes | Immutable | Identifies this governed version |
| Parent Knowledge Asset Identity | Yes | Immutable | Establishes ownership by the parent asset |
| Version Sequence | Yes | Immutable | Establishes ordering within version history |

The exact physical identifier mechanism remains open.

---

## 15.2 Version-Specific Knowledge

A version represents the governed state of the knowledge at that point in time.

Logical version-specific attributes include:

- Knowledge Representation;
- Scope where applicable;
- Structured Knowledge Components;
- Change Context.

These represent the state of the Knowledge Asset at that version.

---

## 15.3 Change Context

A version should preserve why the version exists.

Potential governed revision contexts include:

- new knowledge;
- substantive correction;
- evidence update;
- guideline change;
- safety update;
- taxonomy-driven revision;
- dependency-driven revision;
- other governed revision reason.

The final controlled vocabulary remains to be derived from the governing standards.

---

## 15.4 Lifecycle State

The version has a current governed lifecycle state.

The complete history of lifecycle transitions remains represented through appropriate governance/audit structures rather than duplicated as an uncontrolled history field.

---

## 15.5 Effective and Temporal Information

Version representation must support temporal meaning such as:

- effective state;
- publication timing where applicable;
- supersession timing where applicable;
- other semantically meaningful temporal information.

The detailed event/timestamp model remains subject to further lifecycle and governance design.

---

## 15.6 Governance References

A Knowledge Asset Version may logically reference:

- Review Records;
- Approval Records;
- Provenance Records;
- relevant Governance Records.

These remain separate governed structures.

---

## 15.7 Version-Level Exclusions

The following are not intrinsic Knowledge Asset Version attributes:

- Medical Concept identity;
- Taxonomy Concept identity;
- Source identity;
- Evidence identity;
- Knowledge Relationship identity;
- Dependency identity;
- Review Record itself;
- Approval Record itself;
- Audit history;
- search-index identifiers;
- vector identifiers;
- website URLs;
- social-media URLs;
- database implementation identifiers.

---

# 16. Taxonomy Concept Attributes

## 16.1 Identity

| Attribute | Required | Nature | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taxonomy Concept ID | Yes | Stable / Immutable | Stable concept identity |
| Concept Type | Yes | Governed | Controlled concept classification |
| Concept Status | Yes | Governed | Concept governance state |

The exact identifier syntax remains an implementation-level decision unless constrained by MTS.

---

## 16.2 Controlled Representation

Logical concept representation may include:

- Preferred Term;
- Definition / Concept Description where applicable;
- Synonyms;
- Abbreviations;
- Alternative Terms;
- Historical Terms.

Terminology is governed and must remain separate from concept identity.

---

## 16.3 Multilingual Representation

A Taxonomy Concept has one stable conceptual identity and may have multiple language-specific representations.

Example:

    Taxonomy Concept
        |
        +-- English: Polycystic Ovary Syndrome
        +-- Bangla: পলিসিস্টিক ওভারি সিনড্রোম
        +-- Abbreviation: PCOS

Language representation does not create duplicate concepts merely because the language differs.

---

## 16.4 Concept Type

Concept Type is a governed classification and is not arbitrary free text.

The initial MTS concept types include:

1. Condition
2. Symptom / Sign
3. Finding
4. Anatomy
5. Procedure / Intervention
6. Investigation / Diagnostic Test
7. Medication / Therapeutic Agent
8. Risk Factor
9. Prevention / Health Concept
10. Specialty
11. Other Controlled Medical Concept

The controlled type vocabulary is governed by MTS.

---

## 16.5 Classification

Classification is represented through logical relationships rather than simple scalar attributes.

A Taxonomy Concept may therefore participate in:

- parent/child classification;
- broader/narrower classification;
- taxonomy-specific classification relationships.

Classification relationships must remain distinct from richer semantic or knowledge relationships.

---

## 16.6 Specialty Association

Specialty associations are relationships, not a single scalar specialty attribute.

A Taxonomy Concept may have meaningful relationships with one or more specialties.

---

## 16.7 Concept Boundary

Each Taxonomy Concept must have a clear conceptual boundary.

The model must avoid:

- excessively broad concepts;
- excessively narrow concepts;
- ambiguous concepts;
- duplicate concepts;
- webpage-dependent concept identity.

The exact structure of concept boundary representation remains subject to detailed taxonomy design.

---

## 16.8 Concept Governance

A Taxonomy Concept may participate in governance states such as:

- active;
- deprecated;
- superseded/replaced where applicable.

Taxonomy Concept governance is distinct from Knowledge Asset lifecycle governance.

It follows MTS taxonomy governance rather than KLS Knowledge Asset lifecycle semantics.

---

## 16.9 External Terminology Mapping

External terminology mappings remain separate logical structures.

A Taxonomy Concept may reference external terminology mappings without making the external terminology identifier part of its core identity.

---

# 17. Taxonomy Concept Exclusions

The following do not belong to the core Taxonomy Concept entity:

- Knowledge Asset content;
- Knowledge Asset Version content;
- Evidence;
- Source records;
- medical claims;
- SEO keywords;
- website URLs;
- website navigation;
- publication records;
- audit history;
- generic Knowledge Relationships.

Taxonomy classification does not itself establish medical truth.

|---|---|

> **Knowledge Asset = governed knowledge**

> **Knowledge Asset Version = governed state of that knowledge**

> **Taxonomy Concept = governed medical subject**

# 19. Approved Architectural Decisions

The following decisions are now treated as established architectural direction for P2-02.2 unless explicitly superseded by a later approved decision.

### D-001 — Knowledge Asset Identity

A Knowledge Asset represents a stable conceptual identity for a governed unit of medical knowledge.

### D-002 — Knowledge Asset Version Identity

A Knowledge Asset Version represents a specific governed state of a Knowledge Asset and must have its own stable identity.

### D-003 — Taxonomy Concept Identity

A Taxonomy Concept represents a stable conceptual classification entity and must remain distinct from Knowledge Asset identity.

### D-004 — Asset and Version Separation

Knowledge Asset identity and Knowledge Asset Version identity must remain separate.

Substantive changes to a Knowledge Asset create a new Version rather than a new Knowledge Asset identity.

### D-005 — Version Immutability

Once a Knowledge Asset Version becomes an approved governed state, its substantive semantic content must remain immutable.

Corrections or substantive changes must be represented through a subsequent governed Version or another explicitly approved historical mechanism.

### D-006 — Historical Addressability

Historical Knowledge Asset Versions must remain independently addressable and reconstructable.

The existence of a newer Version must not destroy the ability to identify or reconstruct an earlier governed Version.

### D-007 — Current Version Resolution

A Knowledge Asset may resolve to one current governed Version.

Currentness is a state-resolution concept and must not alter the identity or historical content of Versions.

### D-008 — Lifecycle and Governance Separation

Knowledge lifecycle state and governance events must remain distinguishable from the substantive semantic content of a Knowledge Asset Version.

### D-009 — Substantive versus Operational Change

Substantive semantic changes must be capable of producing a new Knowledge Asset Version.

Operational, editorial, or otherwise non-substantive changes may follow a separate governed policy where explicitly defined.

### D-010 — Multilingual Representation

Multiple language representations may belong to the same Knowledge Asset Version where they represent the same governed knowledge.

Language must not, by itself, create a separate Knowledge Asset identity.

### D-011 — Taxonomy Classification Separation

Taxonomy classification must remain structurally distinct from Knowledge Asset identity and Knowledge Asset Version identity.

Changing classification must not automatically imply creation of a new Knowledge Asset Version.

### D-012 — Relationship Separation

Knowledge relationships must remain explicit and structurally distinguishable from the Knowledge Asset and its Version identity.

Relationships must not be silently embedded as uncontrolled attributes of a Knowledge Asset.

### D-013 — Semantic Relationship Significance

Relationships that materially contribute to the governed meaning or safety of a Knowledge Asset must be capable of being represented as governed semantic relationships.

### D-014 — Evidence and Source Separation

Evidence and Evidence Source must remain distinct logical concepts.

A Source represents the origin or reference from which evidence may be derived or evaluated; it is not itself necessarily the evidence claim.

### D-015 — Evidence Claim Provenance

Evidence Claims must retain sufficient provenance to identify their supporting or contradicting sources and their relationship to the governed Knowledge Asset Version where applicable.

### D-016 — Evidence Strength and Knowledge Confidence Separation

Evidence Strength and Knowledge Confidence must remain distinct concepts.

Evidence Strength describes the strength or quality of supporting evidence, while Knowledge Confidence represents the governed confidence assigned to the knowledge.

They must not be collapsed into a single field or concept.

### D-017 — Conflicting Evidence Preservation

The logical model must be capable of representing conflicting, supporting, or partially supporting evidence without requiring premature destruction or replacement of competing evidence.

### D-018 — Historical Evidence Reconstruction

Historical Knowledge Asset Versions must remain reconstructable with the evidence and provenance state applicable to their governed state, subject to the final detailed Evidence and Governance models.

### D-019 — Governance History Reconstruction

Governance history must remain sufficiently reconstructable to establish material lifecycle and decision events affecting a Knowledge Asset Version.

### D-020 — Explicit Approval

Approval must be represented as an explicit governance event or governed decision rather than inferred solely from the existence of content.

### D-021 — Supersession and Retirement

Supersession and retirement must preserve historical identity and must not require destructive deletion of previously governed Knowledge Asset Versions.

### D-022 — AI Authority Boundary

AI-generated information must not become authoritative knowledge solely because it was generated by AI.

AI may assist with research, extraction, synthesis, translation, classification, or other knowledge operations, but authoritative knowledge requires the applicable verification and governance process.

### D-023 — Logical and Physical Architecture Separation

P2-02.2 defines the logical data model and must not prematurely prescribe database engines, physical tables, indexes, storage technologies, APIs, search infrastructure, vector databases, caching systems, or other implementation-specific mechanisms.

### D-024 — Derived Knowledge and Relationships

Derived or automatically inferred relationships and knowledge structures must not be treated as authoritative knowledge unless explicitly promoted through the applicable governance process.

### D-025 — Governed Semantic Relationship Boundary

Relationships that materially constitute or qualify a governed knowledge assertion represented by a Knowledge Asset Version must be representable as part of that Version's immutable governed semantic state.

Relationships that are contextual, navigational, taxonomy-classification, independently governed, or derived do not automatically require creation of a new Knowledge Asset Version.

A relationship change must therefore be evaluated according to its semantic and governance role rather than by relationship existence alone.

Where a relationship materially changes the governed meaning or safety of a Knowledge Asset, the applicable governance process must determine whether a new Knowledge Asset Version is required.

Derived relationships must not be treated as authoritative knowledge unless explicitly promoted through the applicable governance process.

# 21. Design Constraints

The following constraints are mandatory architectural guardrails for the P2-02.2 logical model and for subsequent implementation work derived from it.

- Do not collapse Knowledge Asset identity and Knowledge Asset Version identity.
- Do not use Version creation as a substitute for creating a genuinely distinct Knowledge Asset.
- Approved Knowledge Asset Versions must remain immutable.
- Historical Knowledge Asset Versions must remain independently addressable and reconstructable.
- The current Version must be resolvable without modifying historical Version identity or content.
- Do not treat currentness as a replacement for historical Version state.
- Do not collapse Knowledge Asset identity with Taxonomy Concept identity.
- Do not treat language representation as a separate Knowledge Asset identity solely because the language differs.
- Taxonomy classification must remain distinct from Knowledge Asset and Knowledge Asset Version identity.
- Knowledge relationships must remain explicit and must not be hidden inside uncontrolled attributes.
- Relationship versioning must be determined by governed semantic significance, not by relationship existence alone.
- Relationships that materially constitute or qualify a governed Knowledge Asset assertion must remain historically reconstructable with the applicable Version.
- Independently governed, contextual, navigational, taxonomy-classification, and derived relationships must not be forced into Knowledge Asset Versioning without an architectural or governance requirement.
- Derived relationships must not become authoritative knowledge without explicit governance.
- Evidence and Evidence Source must remain distinguishable.
- Evidence Claims must retain appropriate provenance.
- Conflicting or materially different evidence must remain representable.
- Evidence Strength must not be used as a substitute for Knowledge Confidence.
- Knowledge Confidence must not be used as a substitute for Evidence Strength.
- Governance events must remain distinguishable from substantive Knowledge Asset content.
- Approval must be reconstructable as an explicit governance decision or event.
- Supersession and retirement must preserve historical identity.
- Historical governance and provenance must not depend solely on mutable current-state fields.
- AI-generated information must not be treated as authoritative without the applicable verification and governance process.
- Derived knowledge must not become authoritative merely because it was algorithmically generated.
- P2-02.2 must remain at the logical data-model level.
- Physical database structures, storage technologies, indexes, APIs, search systems, vector stores, caching systems, and infrastructure mechanisms must not be introduced into this specification unless explicitly required by the logical model.
- Open decisions identified in Section 20 must remain explicitly open until resolved through the appropriate architectural process.
