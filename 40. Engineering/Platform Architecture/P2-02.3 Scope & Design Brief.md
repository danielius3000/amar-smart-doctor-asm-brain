# 1. Purpose

This document defines the scope, objectives, boundaries, inputs, expected outputs, and design principles for **P2-02.3 — Physical Data Design**.

P2-02.3 is the physical-design stage of the P2-02 Knowledge Data Layer milestone.

Its purpose is to translate the approved logical data model established by P2-02.2 into a sufficiently detailed physical data design that can support implementation.

P2-02.3 must preserve the architectural boundaries established by:

- S1-04 — Knowledge Foundation
- P2-01 — Knowledge Platform Architecture
- P2-02.1 — Knowledge Data Layer Architecture & Data Model Foundation
- P2-02.2 — Detailed Knowledge Data Model Specification v1.0

P2-02.3 must not redefine the approved logical model merely to accommodate a preferred database technology or implementation convenience.

# 3. Objective

The objective of P2-02.3 is to establish a governed physical data design that:

1. translates the approved logical model into implementable physical structures;
2. preserves logical entity identity and boundaries;
3. preserves versioning and lifecycle semantics;
4. supports governance and auditability;
5. supports evidence and provenance requirements;
6. supports multilingual knowledge representation;
7. provides appropriate integrity constraints;
8. provides appropriate indexing and access structures;
9. establishes a practical storage strategy;
10. supports future API, search, AI, and knowledge-retrieval requirements without prematurely implementing those systems;
11. provides sufficient implementation detail for controlled engineering execution.

# 5. Core Physical Design Principle

The physical design must follow this direction:

    Governance Standards
            ↓
    Platform Architecture
            ↓
    Data-Layer Architecture
            ↓
    Approved Logical Data Model
            ↓
    Physical Data Design
            ↓
    Implementation

Physical implementation must not drive the logical model.

A database technology, ORM, indexing strategy, or storage optimization must not silently change the meaning of the governed knowledge model.

Where a physical constraint requires a logical-model change, the change must return to the appropriate architectural or logical-model review rather than being introduced silently within physical design.

# 6. Scope

P2-02.3 physical design is expected to address the following areas.

## 6.1 Physical Entity Mapping

Define how the approved logical entities map to physical structures.

At minimum:

- Knowledge Asset
- Knowledge Asset Version
- Taxonomy Concept

The physical design must document the relationship between logical entities and their physical representations.

---

## 6.2 Supporting Data Structures

Define physical structures required to support the governed knowledge model.

These may include structures supporting:

- evidence;
- sources;
- relationships;
- dependencies;
- review;
- approval;
- governance;
- provenance;
- audit;
- taxonomy relationships;
- multilingual representation.

Supporting structures must be justified by requirements.

New physical structures must not be introduced simply because they are conventional database patterns.

---

## 6.3 Physical Identifiers

Define the physical representation of identifiers.

The design must address:

- primary identifiers;
- foreign identifiers;
- uniqueness requirements;
- stable identifiers;
- identifier generation strategy;
- identifier immutability where required.

Physical identifier choices must preserve the identity semantics defined by P2-02.2.

---

## 6.4 Physical Versioning

Define how the logical versioning model is represented physically.

The design should address:

- Knowledge Asset identity;
- Knowledge Asset Version identity;
- version numbering;
- current-version resolution;
- historical versions;
- approved versions;
- superseded versions;
- relationships between asset identity and versions.

Historical reconstruction must remain possible where required by the governing lifecycle and governance model.

---

## 6.5 Lifecycle and Governance State

Define physical support for lifecycle and governance states.

The design should support:

- draft;
- review;
- approved;
- published;
- deprecated;
- retired;
- superseded;
- other governed lifecycle states where applicable.

The physical design must not create lifecycle states that are not supported by the governing standards.

---

## 6.6 Physical Relationships

Define how approved logical relationships are represented physically.

This may include:

- foreign-key relationships;
- relationship tables;
- associative structures;
- relationship metadata;
- relationship integrity constraints.

The physical design must preserve the logical relationship semantics established by P2-02.2.

---

## 6.7 Evidence and Provenance

Define physical structures required to support evidence and provenance.

The design should preserve:

- evidence identity;
- source identity;
- provenance;
- evidence-to-knowledge relationships;
- source attribution;
- relevant temporal information.

Physical design must not weaken evidence traceability.

---

## 6.8 Integrity Constraints

Define appropriate physical integrity mechanisms.

These may include:

- primary-key constraints;
- foreign-key constraints;
- uniqueness constraints;
- nullability constraints;
- check constraints;
- referential integrity;
- lifecycle-related constraints;
- version-related constraints.

Constraints should enforce genuine domain rules rather than implementation preferences.

---

## 6.9 Indexing

Define an initial indexing strategy based on expected access patterns.

The design should consider:

- primary lookups;
- identifier lookups;
- current-version retrieval;
- taxonomy traversal;
- relationship retrieval;
- lifecycle filtering;
- governance filtering;
- evidence retrieval;
- common administrative queries.

Indexes must be justified by expected access patterns and must not be added indiscriminately.

---

## 6.10 Storage Strategy

Define the physical storage approach appropriate to the approved architecture.

This may include:

- relational storage;
- structured document storage where justified;
- specialized storage components where required.

The design must avoid unnecessary polyglot persistence.

A separate storage technology should be introduced only where a clear requirement justifies it.

---

## 6.11 Migration and Initialization

Define the approach for creating and initializing the physical data layer.

Where required, this should address:

- schema initialization;
- baseline data;
- taxonomy initialization;
- migration strategy;
- versioned migrations;
- rollback considerations;
- environment consistency.

The design should distinguish between:

- schema migration;
- reference-data initialization;
- governed knowledge ingestion.

---

## 6.12 Operational Considerations

The physical design should identify relevant operational requirements, including:

- backup considerations;
- restoration requirements;
- data integrity;
- transaction boundaries;
- concurrency considerations;
- monitoring requirements;
- retention considerations.

Detailed production operations may remain outside P2-02.3 where they belong to a later infrastructure or operations milestone.

---

# 7. Out of Scope

P2-02.3 must not expand into unrelated platform implementation.

The following are not part of the initial physical data-design scope unless explicitly required by an approved dependency or decision.

## 7.1 API Implementation

P2-02.3 does not implement:

- REST APIs;
- GraphQL APIs;
- API authentication;
- API controllers;
- API services.

These belong to later platform/API work.

---

## 7.2 Search Implementation

P2-02.3 does not implement:

- search engines;
- search ranking;
- search indexing pipelines;
- semantic search;
- retrieval pipelines.

Search architecture belongs primarily to P2-05.

Physical data design may identify interfaces or requirements relevant to future search integration.

---

## 7.3 Vector Storage

P2-02.3 does not require implementation of:

- vector databases;
- embedding pipelines;
- vector indexes;
- semantic retrieval infrastructure.

Such requirements must be handled through the appropriate AI/search architecture.

---

## 7.4 Caching Architecture

P2-02.3 does not define the complete caching architecture.

Caching may be considered only where necessary to clarify physical data access requirements.

---

## 7.5 AI Workforce Implementation

P2-02.3 does not implement AI agents, model infrastructure, or AI workforce orchestration.

These belong to P2-04.

---

## 7.6 Public Website Implementation

P2-02.3 does not implement the public website or user-facing knowledge experience.

These belong to Phase 3.

# 9. Design Principles

## 9.1 Logical Model First

The approved logical model is the source of truth for physical translation.

---

## 9.2 Minimum Necessary Complexity

The physical design should use the simplest architecture capable of satisfying the approved requirements.

Complexity must be justified.

---

## 9.3 Governed Knowledge First

Physical structures must support the governance of knowledge rather than merely optimize storage convenience.

---

## 9.4 Stable Identity

Physical implementation must preserve stable governed identity.

Names, labels, or mutable display values must not become the sole identity mechanism.

---

## 9.5 Historical Integrity

The system must preserve required historical information rather than overwriting governed knowledge state without traceability.

---

## 9.6 Requirements-Driven Optimization

Indexes, partitions, denormalization, specialized storage, and other optimizations must be justified by actual requirements or measurable access patterns.

---

## 9.7 Avoid Premature Technology Lock-In

Physical design may select appropriate technologies where necessary, but technology decisions must follow architectural requirements.

The project should not adopt multiple database technologies merely because they are technically available.

---

## 9.8 Future-Ready Without Overengineering

The physical design should accommodate foreseeable requirements without implementing speculative infrastructure.

# 15. Implementation Readiness Boundary

Physical design is not the same as implementation.

The project must not begin production implementation merely because a physical design document exists.

Implementation readiness requires, at minimum:

- approved logical model;
- sufficiently complete physical design;
- resolved critical architecture decisions;
- identified migration strategy;
- identified initialization requirements;
- identified integrity constraints;
- identified implementation dependencies;
- architecture review;
- explicit approval to proceed.

---

# 16. Review Criteria

The P2-02.3 physical design should be reviewed against the following criteria.

### Architecture

- Does it conform to P2-02.1?
- Does it respect P2-01 boundaries?
- Does it follow S1-04 standards?

### Logical Model

- Does it correctly implement P2-02.2?
- Are logical identities preserved?
- Are logical relationships preserved?
- Are lifecycle and version semantics preserved?

### Physical Design

- Are physical structures sufficiently defined?
- Are constraints appropriate?
- Is the indexing strategy justified?
- Is the storage strategy practical?
- Is migration sufficiently defined?

### Maintainability

- Is the design understandable?
- Is unnecessary complexity avoided?
- Can the physical design evolve safely?

### Implementation Readiness

- Could an engineering team implement the design without making undocumented architectural decisions?

# 18. Open Decisions

The following decisions must be resolved through evidence and requirements rather than assumption.

Potential areas include:

- database engine selection;
- normalization boundaries;
- physical identifier technology;
- indexing strategy;
- transaction boundaries;
- migration tooling;
- taxonomy storage strategy;
- relationship storage strategy;
- evidence storage strategy;
- audit storage strategy;
- archival strategy;
- retention requirements.

Each decision should record:

- decision;
- rationale;
- alternatives considered;
- consequences;
- governing requirement or architectural input.

---

# 19. Decision Discipline

Physical design must not become a place where unresolved architectural questions are silently decided.

Where a decision affects the approved logical model or platform architecture:

1. identify the conflict;
2. document the issue;
3. determine the appropriate authority;
4. obtain the required review;
5. update the governing artifact if approved;
6. then continue physical design.

This preserves ASM Brain as the project's source of truth.

# 20. Risks

The following risks should be actively monitored.

## 20.1 Physical Design Driving Logical Design

**Risk:** Database convenience causes changes to the governed logical model.

**Control:** P2-02.2 remains authoritative for logical semantics.

---

## 20.2 Premature Technology Selection

**Risk:** Technology is selected before requirements are sufficiently understood.

**Control:** Make technology decisions only where justified by the approved architecture and physical requirements.

---

## 20.3 Overengineering

**Risk:** Multiple storage technologies, excessive indexes, or unnecessary abstractions are introduced.

**Control:** Apply minimum necessary complexity.

---

## 20.4 Loss of Historical Information

**Risk:** Physical design makes historical reconstruction difficult or impossible.

**Control:** Preserve approved versioning and lifecycle semantics.

---

## 20.5 Weak Evidence Traceability

**Risk:** Evidence or provenance cannot be reliably traced to governed knowledge.

**Control:** Preserve evidence and provenance relationships in the physical design.

---

## 20.6 Implementation Before Approval

**Risk:** Engineering begins building against an incomplete physical model.

**Control:** Maintain the implementation-readiness boundary and explicit approval gate.

# 23. Final Design Principle

The Knowledge Data Layer is the structured memory foundation of Amar Smart Doctor.

The physical design must therefore optimize first for:

**correctness → governance → integrity → maintainability → appropriate performance → future scalability**

rather than optimizing prematurely for technology, theoretical scale, or implementation convenience.

The physical database is an implementation of the knowledge architecture.

It is not the architecture itself.
