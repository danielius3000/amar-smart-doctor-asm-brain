# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the architectural and data-model foundation for the Amar Smart Doctor Knowledge Data Layer.

P2-02.1 translates the approved P2-01 Knowledge Platform Architecture into the architectural foundation for the Knowledge Data Layer.

P2-02.1 establishes the architectural boundaries, responsibilities, data domains, governance principles, and logical structure required for subsequent detailed data-model and physical-design work.

The detailed logical data model is defined separately in **P2-02.2 — Detailed Knowledge Data Model Specification**.

P2-02.1 therefore does not duplicate or supersede the approved logical entity definitions established by P2-02.2.

The purpose is to establish sufficient clarity for subsequent P2-02 data architecture, schema design, persistence design, and implementation without prematurely committing the project to a specific database technology or physical schema.

This document therefore defines:

- the purpose of the Knowledge Data Layer
- its architectural position
- primary data domains
- core knowledge objects
- logical relationships
- governance state representation
- evidence and provenance representation
- lifecycle and versioning representation
- dependency representation
- data ownership boundaries
- data integrity principles
- implementation constraints
- unresolved design decisions for subsequent P2-02 work

This document does not define the final physical database schema.

# 4. P2-02 Objective

The objective of P2-02 is to establish a governed Knowledge Data Layer capable of storing and maintaining the structured representation of Amar Smart Doctor knowledge.

The data layer must support the long-term principle:

> Knowledge is created once, governed continuously, and reused across multiple products and experiences.

The data layer must therefore be designed around governed knowledge rather than around website pages or individual content formats.

# 6. Core Architectural Principle

The Knowledge Data Layer must represent governed knowledge as a connected system rather than as isolated documents.

A Knowledge Asset must not exist as an orphaned content record without its relevant governance context.

Where applicable, the platform must be able to connect a Knowledge Asset to:

- the concepts it represents
- its taxonomy classification
- the evidence supporting it
- the sources from which evidence is derived
- relationships with other Knowledge Assets
- dependencies
- lifecycle state
- version history
- review and approval state
- relevant governance metadata

The data architecture must preserve these relationships as first-class information.

# 7. Primary Data Domains

The Knowledge Data Layer is organized around the following logical data domains.

## 7.1 Knowledge Asset Domain

Represents governed Knowledge Assets.

Primary responsibilities include:

- asset identity
- asset type
- title or canonical name
- content representation
- metadata
- authority state
- lifecycle state
- version reference
- review state
- provenance references

The Knowledge Asset remains the central governed knowledge object.

---

## 7.2 Medical / Taxonomy Concept Domain

Represents medical concepts used by the Knowledge System.

Examples may include:

- diseases
- symptoms
- causes
- risk factors
- diagnostic concepts
- treatments
- medicines
- procedures
- anatomical concepts
- specialties
- other governed medical concepts

Medical concepts must remain distinct from Knowledge Assets.

A Knowledge Asset may describe or reference one or more medical concepts, but a concept itself is not automatically a Knowledge Asset.

Within the approved logical data model, the governed logical concept entity is **Taxonomy Concept**, as defined by the Medical Taxonomy Standard (MTS).

The term **Medical Concept** may continue to be used as a broader domain term, but P2-02 does not introduce a separate generic `MedicalConcept` entity unless a future governing requirement explicitly requires one.

---

## 7.3 Taxonomy Domain

Represents classification and semantic organization of medical concepts according to the Medical Taxonomy Standard.

The taxonomy domain must support:

- concept classification
- parent-child relationships
- taxonomy categories
- concept placement
- controlled taxonomy relationships
- future taxonomy evolution

Taxonomy classification must not be confused with general knowledge relationships.

---

## 7.4 Evidence Domain

Represents evidence supporting governed medical knowledge.

The evidence domain must support:

- evidence identity
- evidence type
- evidence metadata
- evidence assessment
- evidence relationship to Knowledge Assets
- evidence relationship to sources
- verification information
- confidence or other governed evidence attributes where defined by ES

The Evidence Standard remains authoritative for evidence governance.

---

## 7.5 Source Domain

Represents external or internal sources from which evidence is derived.

A source may include:

- journal article
- clinical guideline
- systematic review
- official health authority
- medical organization
- textbook
- institutional publication
- other approved evidence source

A source is not itself equivalent to governed knowledge.

The data architecture must preserve the distinction:

Source
↓
Evidence
↓
Governed Knowledge

---

## 7.6 Relationship Domain

Represents governed relationships between knowledge objects.

Relationships may connect:

- Knowledge Assets
- medical concepts
- evidence
- sources
- other governed objects

The relationship domain must follow KDRS.

Relationships must not be treated as arbitrary application-level links.

---

## 7.7 Dependency Domain

Represents dependencies where one governed knowledge object depends on another object for meaning, validity, maintenance, or downstream impact.

Dependency representation must support future impact analysis.

Examples may include:

- a Knowledge Asset depending on a medical concept
- a Knowledge Asset depending on evidence
- a derived Knowledge Asset depending on another governed asset
- an output depending on an approved source or evidence body

Dependency semantics must remain governed by KDRS and related standards.

---

## 7.8 Lifecycle Domain

Represents the controlled lifecycle state of governed knowledge.

The data layer must support lifecycle states established by KLS.

Conceptually:

Draft
↓
Review
↓
Approved
↓
Published
↓
Monitoring
↓
Update
↓
Re-review
↓
Archive

The actual state model must follow the approved KLS specification.

---

## 7.9 Version Domain

Represents controlled versions of governed knowledge.

Versioning must preserve:

- version identity
- version sequence
- relationship to the Knowledge Asset
- creation information
- effective state
- change context
- historical traceability

Versioning responsibilities must remain consistent with KAS, KLS, and KDRS.

The project must not create an independent competing version-governance model.

---

## 7.10 Governance Domain

Represents information required to enforce knowledge governance.

Potential governance information includes:

- ownership
- authority
- review state
- approval state
- publication state
- reviewer information
- approval information
- audit metadata
- governance timestamps
- applicable governance rules

Governance metadata must support the authority boundaries defined by S1-04.

---

# 8. Core Logical Objects

The approved foundational logical entity model is defined in P2-02.2.

The three foundational logical entities are:

1. **Knowledge Asset**
2. **Knowledge Asset Version**
3. **Taxonomy Concept**

P2-02.1 additionally recognizes supporting architectural domains and structures required for evidence, provenance, relationships, dependencies, lifecycle, review, approval, governance, and auditability.

These supporting structures must not be interpreted as a competing core entity model.

## 8.1 Knowledge Asset

The primary governed knowledge object.

Conceptual responsibilities:

- identity
- canonical metadata
- asset classification
- governed content
- status
- lifecycle
- version reference
- provenance
- relationships

---

## 8.2 Knowledge Asset Version

Represents a controlled version of a Knowledge Asset.

A Knowledge Asset may have multiple historical versions.

Version history must be preserved rather than overwritten.

A Knowledge Asset represents the stable identity of a governed knowledge object across its lifecycle. Knowledge Asset Versions represent the governed states of that asset over time and must not be treated as independent Knowledge Assets.

---

## 8.3 Taxonomy Concept

The governed logical concept entity is the Taxonomy Concept defined by the Medical Taxonomy Standard (MTS).

The broader term Medical Concept may be used when referring to the domain concept generally.

The detailed logical definition of Taxonomy Concept is established by P2-02.2.

---

## 8.4 Taxonomy Relationship

Represents the classification relationship between medical concepts and taxonomy structures.

Taxonomy relationships must remain distinct from general semantic relationships.

---

## 8.5 Evidence Record

Represents a governed evidence object supporting a Knowledge Asset or assertion.

---

## 8.6 Source Record

Represents the source associated with an evidence record.

---

## 8.7 Knowledge Relationship

Represents a governed relationship between knowledge objects.

Relationship semantics must follow KDRS.

---

## 8.8 Dependency Record

Represents a governed dependency between objects.

Dependencies must remain distinguishable from ordinary relationships.

---

## 8.9 Review Record

Represents a review event or review state associated with governed knowledge.

The final review structure must follow the governance requirements of the Knowledge System.

---

## 8.10 Approval Record

Represents approval information required before knowledge enters an approved state.

Approval must not be inferred merely from content creation.

---

## 8.11 Governance Record

Represents relevant governance metadata required to maintain authority, ownership, traceability, and control.

# 9. Logical Relationship Model

The following represents the architectural relationship model. The detailed cardinalities, attributes, identity semantics, and logical constraints are governed by P2-02.2.

Knowledge Asset
├── has Knowledge Asset Versions
├── relates to Taxonomy Concepts
├── is supported by governed Evidence
├── is connected to Sources through Evidence
├── relates to other Knowledge Assets
├── may depend on governed objects
├── participates in lifecycle governance
├── participates in review and approval
└── carries governed metadata

This structure should be treated as the conceptual foundation of the data model.

---

# 10. Knowledge Asset and Concept Separation

The data architecture must explicitly preserve the distinction between a Knowledge Asset and a medical concept.

For example:

A medical concept may be:

"Hypertension"

A Knowledge Asset may be:

"Understanding Hypertension: Symptoms, Diagnosis and Treatment"

The Knowledge Asset describes or uses the concept.

The concept remains independently identifiable so that multiple Knowledge Assets can reference it consistently.

This separation is necessary for:

- taxonomy
- search
- relationships
- reuse
- multilingual representation
- knowledge graph capabilities
- future AI retrieval

---

# 11. Knowledge Asset and Content Separation

The platform must also preserve the distinction between governed knowledge and presentation-specific content.

A Knowledge Asset should not be designed exclusively around:

- an HTML page
- a website article
- a social media post
- a video script
- a mobile screen
- an SEO page

These are potential derived experiences or outputs.

The authoritative knowledge must remain independent of any one presentation format.

---

# 12. Evidence and Source Separation

The data model must preserve three distinct concepts:

Source

The original information source.

Evidence
The governed evidence representation supporting a Knowledge Asset or governed knowledge assertion, according to the Evidence Standard.

The detailed logical evidence model and its association boundaries are governed by the approved P2-02 logical model and subsequent Evidence Standard analysis.

Knowledge Asset

The governed knowledge produced through the Knowledge System.

The relationship is:

Source
↓
Evidence
↓
Knowledge Asset

A Knowledge Asset must not treat a source URL or citation as equivalent to evidence governance.

---

# 13. Relationship and Dependency Separation

Relationships and dependencies must remain logically distinct.

A relationship represents a governed connection between knowledge objects.

A dependency represents a condition in which one object relies on another object in a way that may affect validity, maintenance, interpretation, or downstream impact.

This distinction is important for future:

- impact analysis
- lifecycle propagation
- change management
- knowledge graph capabilities
- dependency analysis

KDRS remains authoritative for the detailed relationship and dependency semantics.

---

# 14. Lifecycle Representation

The data model must represent lifecycle state explicitly.

Lifecycle state must not be inferred solely from:

- timestamps
- presence of content
- publication status on a website
- database record existence

A Knowledge Asset should have an explicit governed lifecycle state.

This enables controlled transitions and prevents uncontrolled editing from becoming an implicit lifecycle mechanism.

---

# 15. Version Representation

Version history must be retained.

The data architecture must avoid destructive overwriting of governed knowledge where version history is required.

A conceptual structure is:

Knowledge Asset
├── Version 1
├── Version 2
├── Version 3
└── Current Approved Version

The logical current-version resolution semantics are established in P2-02.2. The physical implementation mechanism will be determined during P2-02.3 Physical Data Design.

---

# 16. Review and Approval Representation

Review and approval must be represented separately from content authorship.

Creating or modifying a Knowledge Asset does not automatically make it approved.

The data layer must therefore support controlled distinction between:

- authoring
- review
- approval
- publication

This preserves the human governance model established by the Knowledge Foundation.

Approval of a Knowledge Asset does not automatically mean that the asset is published to a particular product or public experience. Publication remains a downstream controlled operation governed by lifecycle and product-specific rules.

---

# 17. Provenance

The data layer must preserve provenance information sufficient to understand where governed knowledge originated and how it was developed.

Provenance may include:

- source references
- evidence references
- creation context
- contributor information
- review information
- approval information
- transformation history where applicable

Provenance requirements must remain consistent with ES, KAS, KEF, and KLS.

---

# 18. Auditability

The Knowledge Data Layer must support auditability.

The system should be able to establish:

- what changed
- when it changed
- which object changed
- which version resulted
- who or what initiated the change
- what review occurred
- what approval occurred
- what dependencies or relationships were affected where required

Auditability is a governance requirement, not merely an operational convenience.

---

# 19. Identity Strategy

Core governed objects must have stable identifiers.

At minimum, identifiers should exist for:

- Knowledge Assets
- Knowledge Asset Versions
- Taxonomy Concepts
- Evidence Records
- Sources
- Relationships
- Dependencies
- Reviews
- Approvals

Identifiers must remain stable enough to support references across the platform.

Human-readable names must not be used as the sole identity mechanism.

P2-02.2 establishes the logical identity semantics for Knowledge Assets, Knowledge Asset Versions, and Taxonomy Concepts. Physical identifier technology remains deferred to P2-02.3.

---

# 20. Metadata Principles

Metadata must be structured rather than embedded only inside free-form content.

Potential metadata categories include:

- object identity
- object type
- title
- language
- status
- lifecycle state
- version
- ownership
- timestamps
- provenance
- evidence state
- review state
- approval state
- taxonomy classification
- relationship information
- governance information

The final metadata schema will be defined during detailed P2-02 schema design.

The logical metadata boundary must remain consistent with P2-02.2. Physical storage representation, indexing, serialization, and implementation-specific metadata are deferred to P2-02.3.

---

# 21. Language and Multilingual Readiness

The data model should be capable of supporting multilingual knowledge without duplicating the underlying medical concept unnecessarily.

The architecture should distinguish between:

- canonical concept identity
- language-specific labels
- language-specific representations
- translated or localized content

The project should design for multilingual expansion without implementing unnecessary global complexity during P2-02.

Bangla and English are strategically important product languages, but multilingual implementation details should remain requirements-driven.

Multilingual representation must preserve stable Knowledge Asset and Taxonomy Concept identity across languages. Language-specific representation must not automatically create a new governed identity.

---

# 22. Data Ownership Boundaries

The Knowledge Data Layer owns governed knowledge data.

It does not automatically own all data used by Amar Smart Doctor products.

Examples of data that should remain product-owned may include:

- appointment transactions
- user preferences
- product-specific workflows
- marketing campaign data
- product analytics
- operational records
- future patient-management data

Product systems may reference governed knowledge through approved platform interfaces.

They should not duplicate authoritative knowledge as independent sources of truth.

---

# 23. Product Data Boundary

The relationship should remain:

Product Data
↓
uses
↓
Knowledge Platform
↓
Governed Knowledge

Not:

Product Database
↓
becomes
↓
Medical Knowledge Authority

This boundary protects the central architectural principle that the website and future products are consumers of governed knowledge.

---

# 24. AI Data Boundary

AI systems may:

- retrieve knowledge
- transform knowledge
- assist with knowledge engineering
- assist with research
- generate derived content
- analyze knowledge
- propose changes

AI-generated information must not automatically become authoritative Knowledge Assets.

The data layer must therefore preserve governance state independently from AI generation state.

An AI-generated draft and an approved Knowledge Asset are not equivalent states.

---

# 25. Data Integrity Principles

The Knowledge Data Layer should enforce or support the following principles:

1. Governed objects must have stable identities.
2. Required relationships must remain referentially valid.
3. Lifecycle states must be valid according to KLS.
4. Version relationships must remain consistent.
5. Evidence references must remain traceable.
6. Sources must remain distinguishable from evidence.
7. Knowledge Assets must remain distinguishable from medical concepts.
8. Relationships must follow KDRS.
9. Dependencies must remain traceable.
10. Approval state must not be inferred from authorship.
11. Historical versions must remain traceable where required.
12. Governance metadata must not be silently discarded.
13. Deletion must not destroy required governance history.
14. Product content must not silently become authoritative knowledge.

---

# 26. Deletion and Archival Principle

Governed knowledge must not be treated like ordinary application content.

Where historical, governance, evidence, relationship, or audit information must be preserved, destructive deletion should not be used.

Archival and lifecycle mechanisms should be preferred where required by KLS or other governing standards.

The exact retention and deletion policy will be defined through detailed governance and implementation design.

---

# 27. Data Access Principle

The Knowledge Data Layer should not become a directly accessible public database.

Access should occur through governed platform services.

The conceptual access path is:

Products / AI / Internal Tools
↓
Platform Services
↓
Governance Controls
↓
Knowledge Data Layer

Direct uncontrolled writes from products or AI systems must not be permitted where they could bypass governance.

---

# 28. Read and Write Separation

The architecture should distinguish between:

- governed knowledge creation
- governed knowledge modification
- review
- approval
- publication
- retrieval

Read access to approved knowledge may be broader than write access.

Write operations must be controlled according to role, authorization, lifecycle state, and governance requirements.

---

# 30. Candidate Relationship Set

The detailed schema should investigate relationships such as:

- KnowledgeAsset represents MedicalConcept
- MedicalConcept classified by Taxonomy
- KnowledgeAsset supported by EvidenceRecord
- EvidenceRecord derived from Source
- KnowledgeAsset related to KnowledgeAsset
- KnowledgeAsset depends on KnowledgeAsset
- KnowledgeAsset depends on EvidenceRecord
- KnowledgeAsset has KnowledgeAssetVersion
- KnowledgeAsset has LifecycleState
- KnowledgeAsset has ReviewRecord
- KnowledgeAsset has ApprovalRecord
- KnowledgeAsset has GovernanceRecord
- KnowledgeAsset has ProvenanceRecord

Lifecycle state is currently treated as governed state associated with a Knowledge Asset and/or Knowledge Asset Version rather than as an independent logical entity. The final representation will be determined during detailed schema design according to KLS requirements.

These are candidate logical relationships and must be validated against KAS, MTS, ES, KLS, and KDRS during detailed P2-02 design.

P2-02.1 does not yet prescribe whether evidence is associated only at Knowledge Asset level or at a finer-grained assertion/claim level. This will be evaluated during detailed data-model design based on ES requirements and actual knowledge representation needs.

---

# 31. Physical Storage Neutrality

P2-02.1 does not select a database technology.

The physical persistence strategy must be selected only after evaluating:

- data relationships
- transaction requirements
- governance requirements
- query patterns
- search requirements
- scalability
- maintainability
- operational capacity
- security
- cost
- team capability

Technology selection must follow the architecture rather than drive it.

---

# 32. Relational vs Knowledge Graph Consideration

The Knowledge System contains substantial relationships and dependencies.

However, the existence of relationships does not automatically require a dedicated graph database.

The project should first establish the governed logical data model.

A graph-oriented implementation may be introduced later if actual retrieval, traversal, dependency analysis, or scale requirements justify it.

The initial architecture must avoid technology-driven complexity.

---

# 33. Search and Retrieval Boundary

Search and retrieval are downstream consumers of the Knowledge Data Layer.

The data layer should preserve the structures necessary for future:

- structured search
- semantic retrieval
- taxonomy filtering
- relationship traversal
- evidence-aware retrieval
- multilingual retrieval
- knowledge graph capabilities

However, P2-02.1 does not implement the Search & Retrieval milestone.

Search-specific indexing and retrieval infrastructure remain outside the current scope unless explicitly required for the data-layer implementation.

---

# 34. API Boundary

The Knowledge API is a later milestone.

The data layer should be designed so that governed knowledge can eventually be exposed through controlled APIs.

P2-02.1 must not turn database structure into an accidental public API contract.

The API will be defined under P2-03.

---

# 35. Security Boundary

The data layer must support the security requirements established by P2-01.

Security design must consider:

- authentication
- authorization
- least privilege
- write protection
- auditability
- data protection
- administrative access
- governance operations

Detailed security implementation may be addressed during subsequent engineering design.

---

# 36. Governance Boundary

The data layer is responsible for representing and supporting governance.

It is not itself the authority that defines medical governance.

The authority remains:

S1-04 Knowledge Foundation
↓
Governed technical implementation

Technical implementation must not create a competing governance model.

---

# 37. Implementation Constraints

P2-02 implementation must follow these constraints:

1. Do not bypass the Knowledge Foundation.
2. Do not redefine KAS, KEF, MTS, ES, KLS, or KDRS.
3. Do not make the website the authoritative knowledge store.
4. Do not make AI-generated content automatically authoritative.
5. Do not introduce unnecessary microservices.
6. Do not select database technology before requirements justify it.
7. Do not implement future product-specific data structures prematurely.
8. Do not implement the public API as part of the data-layer milestone.
9. Do not implement Search & Retrieval as a substitute for the data-layer architecture.
10. Preserve auditability and governance history where required.
11. Preserve stable identities for governed objects.
12. Preserve historical knowledge states where required.
13. Keep implementation proportional to the current project stage.

Where P2-02 requires a decision not specified by P2-01, the decision must remain consistent with the principles and constraints established by P2-01.

---

# 44. Relationship to S1-04

P2-02.1 operationalizes the data implications of:

- KAS
- KEF
- MTS
- ES
- KLS
- KDRS

The Knowledge Data Layer therefore acts as the technical persistence foundation for the governed Knowledge System.

It does not replace the Knowledge System standards.

---

# 45. Final Architectural Principle

The Knowledge Data Layer should be built as the structured memory of Amar Smart Doctor.

It must preserve not only:

> what the knowledge is

but also:

> what it represents
> what supports it
> where it came from
> how it relates to other knowledge
> what it depends on
> what version it is
> where it is in its lifecycle
> whether it has been reviewed
> whether it has been approved
> and what authority governs it.

The objective is not to build a large database.

The objective is to build a reliable, governed, traceable foundation upon which the future Amar Smart Doctor platform can safely operate.
